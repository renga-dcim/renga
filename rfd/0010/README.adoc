= RFD 10 Power inventory and topology

== Goal

Model how power reaches equipment, from the panel to the power supply, so
operators can answer what loses power when a feed fails, which devices lack
redundant supply, and how close each feed is to its limit. RFD 5 left power
feeds, panels, outlets, and power paths to a later RFD; this is that RFD.

Power follows the same rule as the rest of Renga: planned connections, observed
evidence, and the current topology stay distinct, and evidence never rewires
the plan silently.

== NetBox reference

NetBox models power panels at a site or location, power feeds from a panel to a
rack, power outlets on PDUs, and power ports on devices, joined by cables. A
feed carries its supply type, phase, voltage, amperage, and maximum utilization;
a power port carries its maximum and allocated draw. Renga keeps that shape and
adds observed draw and supply health as evidence.

== Model

* `PowerPanel`: a named panel placed at a site or location from RFD 5.
* `PowerFeed`: one circuit from a panel, with supply (AC or DC), phase (single
  or three), voltage, amperage, maximum utilization (default 80%, editable per
  feed), and a
  redundancy side (`a`, `b`, or none). A feed may be assigned to a rack.
* Power outlets and power ports are typed endpoints created from the RFD 6
  catalog templates (`power_outlet` and `power_port`), the same way interfaces
  come from interface templates. A PDU is an ordinary `pdu` resource with outlet
  templates. A server's power supplies give it power ports.
* A power port carries its maximum draw and allocated draw in watts; an outlet
  carries its feed leg on three-phase PDUs.
* Power connections reuse RFD 7's plan, assertion, and current-cable model with
  a `power` cable kind. A connection joins a power port to an outlet, or a PDU's
  inlet port to a feed.

Every power object gets a resource envelope and typed projection, like the
other DCIM objects.

== Power path

The current power path of a device is the chain from each of its power ports
through an outlet, its PDU, and the PDU's inlet to a feed and panel. Renga
computes it from current power connections; it is never stored as a separate
truth. A port whose chain is incomplete shows where it stops.

Redundancy is a property of the path: a device is redundantly powered when its
ports reach at least two feeds on different sides. A device with two supplies
both on side `a` is not redundant, whatever its supply count says.

== Capacity and utilization

Electrical quantities retain their units: real power in W, apparent power in
VA, and current in A. For AC, usable apparent capacity is voltage × amperage ×
maximum utilization for single-phase, or √3 × line-to-line voltage × line
current × maximum utilization for balanced three-phase. Single-phase voltage
is measured across the supply conductors. DC capacity is voltage × amperage ×
maximum utilization in W. The configured amperage is the circuit's rated
current, not an observed load.

Allocated real load sums the allocated input watts of ports on the feed's path.
AC conversion from W to VA requires an explicit per-load power factor
(VA = W / power factor, with 0 < power factor <= 1), including its provenance
as configured or measured. Missing power factor leaves allocated apparent load
and current headroom unknown; Renga does not silently assume unity or compare
watts directly with VA. An owner may set one organization-wide default power
factor; until then no default exists. Headroom computed from that default is
labelled as resting on a configured default rather than a per-load value.
Measured W, VA, current, and power factor remain
separate evidence, with their measurement time and coverage.

Renga shows allocated and measured load side by side, comparing only compatible
quantities. Watt totals alone are not verified circuit headroom or assurance
that another server can safely be connected. Three-phase PDUs show current and
allocated load per leg using that leg's voltage and load assignment; the
balanced aggregate formula cannot establish per-leg headroom. Missing leg
assignments or conversion data show incomplete capacity, not spare capacity.

== Entered, discovered, and computed

Nothing on the network knows a building's electrical layout, so part of the
model is always entered. Evidence fills in the rest, and Renga computes what
follows from both.

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Kind |Data

|Entered
|Panels and feeds with their supply, phase, voltage, amperage, maximum
utilization, side, and rack; power connections (which outlet each port is
plugged into); allocated draw per port, defaulting from the hardware type's
catalog template; the organization's default power factor.

|Discovered
|Power supplies with model, serial, and rated watts; supply status; host input
power; PDU outlet state and per-outlet and per-leg readings.

|Computed
|Power paths, redundancy, allocated and measured load, headroom, and "what
fails if this feed fails".
|===

Discovery can suggest a power connection (see <<suggested-connections>>) but
never records one.

== Evidence sources

Inventory facts such as installed supplies arrive through observations. Timed
power readings use the telemetry batches defined below, and every fact and
reading records which source produced it.

=== Host agent

The agent runs as an unprivileged account in a systemd sandbox with no
capabilities and no device access. Its power sources respect that:

* **hwmon:** `power*_input` and `power*_average` under `/sys/class/hwmon`, from
  drivers such as `acpi_power_meter` that many servers expose. These files are
  world-readable, so this works in the existing sandbox and is on by default.
* **SMBIOS power supplies:** the firmware's System Power Supply records (type
  39) list each supply's location, vendor, model, serial, rated watts, and
  whether it is hot-swappable. The raw tables are root-only but never change
  until the next boot, so a failure-tolerant privileged pre-start helper reads
  only the fixed `DMI` and `smbios_entry_point` files from
  `/sys/firmware/dmi/tables`. It opens the source and destination without
  following symbolic links, writes both files with explicit `root:renga-agent`
  ownership and `0440` permissions into a new generation under a root-owned,
  non-agent-writable directory, writes the boot ID and generation ID into its
  manifest, then publishes the complete generation with one atomic rename.
  The agent rejects a partial generation or one from another boot. The unit
  uses an ignored-failure `ExecStartPre=-+` command, so an unavailable snapshot
  reports that source unavailable without preventing ordinary agent startup.
  The `+` prefix deliberately runs the helper outside the service's normal
  filesystem sandbox; its fixed source, no-follow opens, and root-controlled
  destination are therefore the security boundary. Nothing is written into
  agent-controlled `/var/lib/renga`. The same snapshot supplies the DMI UUID
  and serial, which the sandboxed agent cannot read today and RFD 11 uses to
  match VMs to their guests. Vendors do not always populate these records;
  empty records report nothing rather than an absent supply.
* **Direct in-band IPMI is not used.** `/dev/ipmi0` accepts caller-selected BMC
  commands, so device permissions cannot enforce sensor-only access. The main
  agent keeps `PrivateDevices=true` and never joins an IPMI group. Operators
  that need in-band DCMI or supply sensors run a locally managed
  `ipmi_exporter`, whose metrics the sandboxed agent reads over the bounded
  exporter interface below. Out-of-band Redfish is collected separately.
* **RAPL is not used.** Its energy counters have been root-only since 2020
  because they leak secrets through a power side channel (CVE-2020-8694), and
  they measure only the CPU package and memory, often well under the supply's
  input. They can never count toward feed capacity. A later opt-in, trend-only
  source stays possible.

Neither the agent nor Renga can grant a source more privilege. Enabling one is
a local operator action, like enabling job capabilities in RFD 9.

=== Local Prometheus exporters

Hosts that already run Prometheus exporters can let the agent read them
instead:

[source,toml]
----
[[exporters]]
url = "http://127.0.0.1:9100/metrics" # node_exporter

[[exporters]]
url = "http://127.0.0.1:9290/metrics" # ipmi_exporter
----

The agent scrapes each endpoint in its own worker and keeps only a fixed,
versioned mapping of known metrics: the DCMI power reading, power-type and
supply sensors from ipmi_exporter, and hwmon power from node_exporter.
Everything else stays in Prometheus. This keeps privilege with the exporter
the operator already configured, leaves the agent's sandbox unchanged, and
needs no Prometheus server; readings travel in telemetry batches tied to the
agent's identity and work behind NAT.

The fetch contract is deliberately narrow:

* HTTP and HTTPS endpoints must resolve to loopback, and the agent verifies the
  actual peer address on every connection. A configured endpoint requires an
  explicit `allow_remote = true` to connect elsewhere. Redirects are disabled,
  proxy environment variables are ignored for loopback endpoints, and a
  remote endpoint is revalidated against the same configured destination
  policy on every connection.
* Connect and total request time, decoded response bytes, parsed series,
  labels per series, and samples per scrape are bounded. Authentication, when
  configured for a remote endpoint, comes from endpoint-specific local files;
  the Renga intake key is never sent to an exporter.
* A timeout, limit violation, unreachable endpoint, or malformed response
  fails that source scrape and never becomes a zero reading.
* When a native source and an exporter produce fresh, valid readings for the
  same target, measurement boundary, quantity, unit, and sampling window, the
  native reading wins and the exporter's is kept as supporting evidence.

Other RFDs may map more exporter metrics through the same configuration.

=== Collectors and BMCs

This RFD owns two read-only collector modules in the existing agent: a
`power_poller` for PDU and UPS protocols, beginning with SNMP, and a
`bmc_power` collector for Redfish. The first measured-load rollout is the PDU
portion of `power_poller`, because it covers every device in a rack and is the
only source of per-outlet and per-leg load. UPS support follows through the
same stream. Redfish reports supply health, input watts, and capacity without
host privilege.

The modules reuse RFD 11's collector framework and contracts for stable
installation-local collector IDs, server-resolved stream identity,
credential-locality, isolated scheduling, per-stream freshness, and queue
replay. RFD 11 itself supplies switch and libvirt modules, not these power
protocols. Until that shared framework exists, measured-load rollout depends
on it; implementing PDU/UPS and Redfish collection remains in RFD 10.

=== Source status

The agent reports every power source in its check-ins as `available`,
`not_permitted`, `not_present`, or `unreachable`. The Collectors page shows that
status beside each agent, with the local exporter or configuration that would
enable a source that is not permitted.

=== Reading identity and eligibility

A power sample has a canonical identity: stable target and sensor identifiers;
the measurement boundary (`host_input`, `supply_input`, `supply_output`,
`outlet`, `feed`, or `leg`); quantity and unit; sampling-window start and end;
the source stream and mapping version; event time; and a validity status. It
also carries server receipt time for audit, but receipt and agent check-in times
never make an old sample fresh. Non-finite values, unit mismatches, failed
scrapes, and explicit missing values are invalid evidence, not zero.

The current-reading projection advances by event time within one source and
sensor series. A replayed or delayed sample is retained for history but cannot
replace a newer current value. Implausibly future event times are invalid.
Every source mapping declares a freshness window; expiration is evaluated from
the sample's event time independently of installation contact, inventory
acceptance, and reconciliation clocks.

Readings from different sources are equivalent only when target, sensor
meaning, measurement boundary, quantity, unit, and sampling window are truly
comparable. Source precedence, including native over exporter, selects among
equivalent readings only while each candidate is valid and fresh. It never
substitutes a supply-output measurement for host input or a rolling average for
an instantaneous value.

Each aggregate declares what it covers. Capacity calculations require fresh,
valid coverage of every contributing load, conversion, and leg; incomplete
coverage yields unknown headroom and can never display positive spare
capacity. One calculation uses either a boundary's aggregate total or its
constituent sensors, never both. Missing, stale, or failed data remains unknown.

Redundancy remains a structural property of confirmed power paths. Live supply
or outlet availability is separate evidence and may open a supply-failed
finding, but it does not rewrite the structural redundancy badge or create a
path.

== Evidence and connections

Evidence never creates or removes a power connection. An outlet reporting draw
with no connected port, or a supply reporting no input, opens a finding that an
operator resolves, as LLDP evidence does for data cables in RFD 7.

[[suggested-connections]]
=== Suggested connections

Which outlet a power supply is plugged into is the hardest fact to enter and
keep current, and readings can point to it. Renga suggests a connection when:

* an outlet's measured draw tracks one supply's input power over a window, and
  no other unconnected supply in the rack tracks it as well; or
* an outlet turns off or loses draw at the same time as one supply loses input.

A suggestion is a finding carrying the readings that support it. Accepting it
records the planned connection as an operator's change; dismissing it
suppresses that pairing. Suggestions are never applied automatically, and an
existing connection is never replaced by one; a contradicting reading opens
the usual mismatch finding instead.

== Measured history

Readings are sampled on their own interval (one minute by default) and
delivered through a versioned telemetry payload. Each batch has a replay ID and
retains the installation, source stream, collector, target, and sensor
identities that produced every sample. A typed telemetry endpoint accepts each
source/replay-ID pair idempotently; its payload cannot be mistaken for an
inventory observation.

The agent persists telemetry in a separate queue namespace whose entries carry
their payload type and destination. Entry, byte, and age limits apply only to
that queue. When backpressure exceeds those limits, it drops the oldest
telemetry batch, reports the lost batch count and time range in queue health,
and never evicts an inventory observation. Delivery drains inventory first and
caps telemetry work per pass, so a telemetry backlog cannot delay inventory or
lease check-ins indefinitely. Telemetry acceptance does not advance inventory
accepted/reconciled clocks, change snapshot completeness, retire inventory, or
trigger reconciliation.

Renga keeps the latest reading of each quantity, with its time, source, and
coverage, in its own current-reading tables using the event-time rules above.
Findings and headroom use only eligible current readings; structural redundancy
uses confirmed paths. A separate bounded correlation window supports connection
suggestions as described above.

History sits behind a `Renga.Telemetry.Store` behaviour, which writes samples,
and reads an organization-scoped time range in fixed buckets. Every bucket
returns average, minimum, maximum, last, and sample count; a bucket with no
samples is a gap and is never interpolated or returned as zero.

* The default adapter is built in and bounded. It keeps five-minute averages in
  Postgres for 30 days and enforces that retention itself, so a self-hosted
  Renga needs no extra infrastructure.
* An external adapter must provide both write transport and a tenant-scoped
  range-query API with the same bucket and gap semantics. Prometheus or
  VictoriaMetrics remote write supplies only ingestion and is incomplete
  without a corresponding PromQL-compatible read path. External retention and
  downsampling are operator-managed backend policy, which the adapter reports
  rather than pretending to enforce. A TimescaleDB adapter can provide both
  sides directly.

The store only feeds charts and trends. Swapping adapters, or losing the store,
never changes current-reading eligibility, a finding, or a capacity figure.
Store queries always include organization scope, and an external adapter must
isolate that scope in its backend namespace and query. The behaviour is not
specific to power; later RFDs may record other readings through it.

== Findings

* **Not redundant**: a device whose power ports do not reach two sides, when its
  hardware type has more than one power port.
* **Feed near capacity**: allocated or measured load exceeds usable capacity in
  compatible units, or a three-phase leg exceeds its current limit or is out of
  balance. Unknown conversion or leg data is shown as incomplete capacity.
* **Supply failed**: a power supply reported failed or without input.
* **Unexpected draw**: an outlet drawing power with no connection recorded.
* **Suggested connection**: readings point to a supply's outlet; see
  <<suggested-connections>>.
* **Path incomplete**: a port connected to an outlet whose PDU has no feed.

Findings join the Inbox under a Power domain and follow the existing workflow.

== User interaction

* A site's power page lists panels and feeds with capacity bars for allocated
  and measured load, and each feed and device shows a measured-load trend from
  the telemetry store.
* A rack shows its feeds, its PDUs' outlets, and which device each outlet
  serves, next to the elevation.
* A device's Hardware tab shows its power ports, their path to a feed, and a
  redundancy badge.
* "What fails if this feed fails" is a feed action listing every device that
  loses all of its supply, and every device that loses its redundancy.

Mutations require the managed-inventory owner/admin policy.

== Initial exclusions

UPS runtime modeling, generator and transfer-switch topology, cooling, power
billing, and automatic outlet control. Switching an outlet would be an `operate`
capability under RFD 9 and needs its own design.

== Decisions from review

* An owner may set an organization-wide default power factor; headroom that
  uses it is labelled as resting on a configured default, and nothing is
  assumed until it is set.
* New feeds default to 80% maximum utilization, editable per feed.
* Measured history sits behind a swappable `Renga.Telemetry.Store` behaviour.
  The built-in adapter keeps five-minute averages for 30 days in Postgres;
  other adapters must provide both tenant-scoped writes and range reads, with
  retention managed by their backend. Findings and capacity use eligible
  current readings, never the chart store.
* Telemetry uses a versioned payload and its own bounded queue and endpoint.
  Replay is idempotent, loss is reported, and telemetry cannot evict or starve
  inventory or alter inventory reconciliation and completeness clocks.
* Readings may suggest which outlet a supply uses, as a finding an operator
  accepts or dismisses; they never record a connection.
* Measured load comes first from the RFD 10 `power_poller`; its PDU/UPS and
  Redfish modules reuse RFD 11's collector contracts but are not supplied by
  RFD 11. Host power supply readings follow.
* The agent's own power sources are hwmon (on by default), an SMBIOS snapshot
  atomically published by a privileged pre-start helper into root-controlled
  storage, and local exporters. Direct `/dev/ipmi0` access and RAPL are not
  used.
* The agent can read local Prometheus exporters, such as node_exporter and
  ipmi_exporter, through a fixed metric mapping and bounded fetch contract.
  Endpoints are loopback by default, and equivalent native readings win over
  exporter readings only while fresh and valid.

== Rollout

. Add panels and feeds at sites and locations, with capacity.
. Create power ports and outlets from catalog templates on existing and new
  assets.
. Add power connections on the RFD 7 cable model and compute power paths.
. Add allocated load, redundancy, and the "what fails" view.
. Add measured load through the RFD 10 PDU collector, then supply evidence from
  host agents: hwmon and the SMBIOS snapshot, local exporters, and the RFD 10
  Redfish collector.
. Add the power findings and the Power Inbox domain.
. Add the telemetry store with its bounded Postgres adapter, and measured-load
  trends.
