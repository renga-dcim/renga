= RFD 5 Physical containment

== Goal

Model where physical inventory belongs: geographic or organizational groups,
sites, nested locations, racks, rack positions, and unplaced resources. Renga
must represent incomplete and conflicting placement evidence without inventing
certainty, while preventing impossible canonical rack occupancy.

This RFD owns physical containment and placement. Hardware dimensions come from
RFD 6. Cables and Layer 2 topology belong to RFD 7. Power feeds, panels,
outlets, and power paths require a later RFD.

== NetBox reference

Renga adopts the useful boundaries from NetBox's
link:https://github.com/netbox-community/netbox/blob/main/netbox/dcim/models/sites.py[site
and location models] and
link:https://github.com/netbox-community/netbox/blob/main/netbox/dcim/models/racks.py[rack
model]: sites represent campuses or facilities, locations form a hierarchy
inside a site, and racks occupy one site and optionally one location. NetBox's
same-site and rack-geometry validation are important invariants.

Renga differs by preserving source assertions and reconciliation decisions.
Discovery or import evidence does not silently become confirmed placement.

== Containment model

The initial typed projections are:

* `SiteGroup`: an optional recursively nested grouping for region, business
  geography, or another organization-defined site hierarchy;
* `Site`: a campus, datacenter, office, or other independently managed facility;
* `Location`: a recursively nested area inside exactly one site, such as a
  building, floor, room, row, cage, or zone;
* `Rack`: a physical rack in exactly one site and optionally one location;
* `DesiredPlacement`: operator intent for a physical resource;
* `CurrentPlacement`: the reconciled current placement projection; and
* `PlacementEvidence`: a source and observation assertion about placement.

Site groups, sites, locations, and racks receive resource envelopes because they
need stable identity, lifecycle, labels, revisions, relationships, and change
history. Add their kinds to the accepted resource vocabulary. Placement and
occupancy remain subordinate typed records rather than resources.

Every object and relationship carries `organization_id`. Composite foreign keys
prevent cross-organization or cross-site containment. A location's parent must
belong to the same site, and a rack's location must belong to the rack's site.
Moving a location subtree to another site is rejected while it contains child
locations, racks, or placements; an explicit transactional move operation may
be designed later.

Recursive site-group and location writes reject self-parenting and cycles. Reads
derive ancestry and descendants with recursive queries rather than trusting a
client-supplied path. Cached depth or path values, if introduced for performance,
are rebuildable projections rather than authoritative relationships.

Hierarchy mutations serialize on an organization-and-hierarchy lock before
checking ancestry and writing the parent. This prevents concurrent inverse-parent
updates from each observing an acyclic snapshot and committing a cycle. Tests
must cover that race for site groups and locations.

== Sites, locations, and racks

A site initially has a name, stable slug, status, description, physical address,
time zone, and metadata. Coordinates and external facility identifiers are
optional typed fields or identifiers when integrations require them.

A location has a name, optional parent, status, description, and metadata. Its
kind is intentionally organization vocabulary rather than a fixed hierarchy:
one deployment may use building/floor/room while another uses campus/cage/row.
Containment, not the kind label, establishes topology.

A rack has a name, status, facility ID, height in rack units, width, starting
unit convention, optional outer dimensions, and metadata. Rack height must be
positive. Width and dimension units use bounded choices. Changing height or
unit convention cannot strand existing occupancy outside the resulting rack.
Geometry and unit-convention mutations acquire the same rack lock used by
current-placement writes, then revalidate every occupancy range before updating
in the same transaction. This prevents a concurrent shrink and placement from
committing out-of-bounds occupancy.

== Placement and occupancy

A physical resource may be unplaced, placed only at a site or location, or
installed in a rack. Rack installation records starting unit, positive rack-unit
height, and face: front, rear, or full-depth. Zero-U and freestanding equipment
may be assigned to a site/location/rack without consuming rack-unit occupancy.

Desired and current placement are separate. A resource may have at most one of
each, allowing confirmed placement in rack A and an intended move to rack B to
coexist. Desired placement is ordinary operator intent. Operator confirmation
and imported or discovered claims are attributed assertions; deterministic
reconciliation selects `CurrentPlacement` while retaining disagreement and
provenance. Site and location are derived or validated against the selected rack
within each layer so three independently editable fields cannot disagree.

Rack occupancy uses subordinate face-specific rows with PostgreSQL integer
ranges. A front- or rear-mounted device consumes one face; a full-depth device
consumes both. An exclusion constraint rejects overlapping unit ranges on the
same rack and face. Only `CurrentPlacement` drives physical occupancy. A desired
placement is checked for feasibility and can produce a blocked-move finding, but
does not reserve units or displace current equipment. Current placement writes
lock the rack, validate bounds, and update placement plus occupancy atomically.
Resizing or moving equipment cannot leave partial occupancy behind.

Hardware height from an assigned device type is the default, not immutable
truth. An explicit placement height supports unknown or exceptional equipment,
and disagreement with the catalog becomes a finding rather than a corrupted
rack range.

== Placement evidence and reconciliation

`PlacementEvidence` links organization, source, observation, resource, asserted
site/location/rack identifiers, optional position/face, confidence, observed
time, and metadata. Source-local facility IDs are mapped through normal resource
identifier claims or explicit source mappings.

Evidence may be complete, partial, or unresolved. Renga records findings for an
unknown location, ambiguous facility identifier, disagreement between sources,
catalog-height mismatch, evidence conflicting with confirmed placement, and a
resource reported in two places at the same effective time.

Agent hostname, IP address, or network adjacency alone is not placement proof.
Inference policies may propose a site or location and must record their inputs
and confidence. They do not replace operator-confirmed placement automatically.
Age can mark evidence stale. Source omission does so only when the source policy
and accepted observation declare placement a complete snapshot; omission from a
partial or capability-limited observation has no absence meaning. Neither case
clears desired placement or another source's current evidence.

== User interaction

The first UI provides site and location navigation, rack lists, rack elevations,
unplaced-resource queues, and placement findings. Rack elevations show front and
rear occupancy, lifecycle, freshness, and whether placement is confirmed,
observed, or inferred.

Routes belong inside the existing `live_session
:require_authenticated_user` block and `[:browser,
:require_authenticated_user]` pipeline because placement is organization
inventory and every view requires login. All context calls accept
`current_scope` first. Read access follows organization membership; placement
mutations require the project's managed-inventory owner/admin policy.

Initial routes are:

* `/dcim/sites`
* `/dcim/sites/:id`
* `/dcim/locations/:id`
* `/dcim/racks`
* `/dcim/racks/:id`
* `/dcim/placement-findings`

== Initial exclusions

The first implementation excludes floor-plan drawing, geofencing, warehouse
stock movement, reservations for future rack occupants, cable paths, power
topology, environmental sensors, and automatic placement from network data.

== Rollout

. Add sites and nested locations with tenant-safe containment.
. Add racks, geometry, and rack elevations.
. Add canonical placement and overlap-safe occupancy.
. Add source placement evidence and reconciliation findings.
. Integrate hardware dimensions from RFD 6 and topology navigation from RFD 7.
