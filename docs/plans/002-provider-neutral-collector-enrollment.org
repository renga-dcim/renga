#+title: Provider-Neutral Collector Enrollment
#+status: Draft

* Objective

Make automated, policy-driven enrollment the default fleet path. An authorized
installation creates the existing Collector aggregate: a durable
=Source(kind: "host_agent")= provenance record and its =Agent= installation
registration. Manual enrollment remains an optional development, static-host,
and break-glass verifier rather than the default UI, packaging, or deployment
workflow.

* Architecture

** Provider-Neutral Lifecycle

Use one enrollment lifecycle with pluggable evidence verifiers. OIDC, SPIFFE,
Kubernetes, AWS, GCP, Azure, TPM/private infrastructure, and manual grants are
evidence sources, not separate lifecycles. The boundary is provider-neutral,
but claims retain verifier-specific meaning; a policy inspecting provider claims
is necessarily provider-specific.

Each installation generates a stable UUID and keypair, obtains a short-lived
challenge, submits evidence, and proves key possession. Renga verifies evidence,
evaluates the organization's deployment-group policy, and transactionally
creates the Source+Agent aggregate, allow decision, and key-bound runtime
credential. That credential serves observations, check-ins, renewal, and
optional re-attestation. No new persisted Collector entity is required absent a
later ownership need. Images contain only a non-secret policy identifier.

Identity is namespaced by organization, immutable verifier-configuration
version, issuer, and subject; a bare subject is never globally unique. Verifiers
declare subject cardinality. A singleton subject (for example, a verified cloud
instance) requires an explicit replacement decision for a different key. Group
subjects may authorize multiple keys under policy limits and rate controls.
Retries proving the same key return the existing enrollment result.

** Trust, Binding, And Atomicity

Keep evidence verification, policy evaluation, and credential issuance
separate. Verification authenticates signatures, issuer, audience, nonce,
freshness, and replay controls and returns normalized claims. Policy decides
whether that subject may perform =collector:enroll=. Issuance binds the accepted
installation key to the new Collector and records the decision.

Challenges are short-lived, single-use, and server-bound to organization,
action, immutable policy and verifier-configuration versions, and key
thumbprint. A policy selector is routing information and cannot select trust
roots. Bind evidence to challenge and key where supported. For bearer formats
that cannot embed them, enforce native audience, freshness, and replay controls,
record lower assurance, and permit policy to reject it; key proof alone does not
bind an unrelated bearer assertion.

Challenge consumption, one-time grant consumption, Collector creation, decision
persistence, and credential issuance are one atomic successful binding. A lost
response is safely retried with the same key. Denials are audited without a
Collector transaction.

** Constrained ABAC And Provenance

Enrollment policy is a default-deny, domain-specific ABAC admission decision,
not evidence verification. Inputs are the normalized subject; action
=collector:enroll=; organization and policy resource; trusted time, challenge,
and request context; installation public key; and untrusted requested
capabilities. Existing organization RBAC protects policy administration;
enrolling agents may evaluate but not author policies. Broader ABAC is deferred.

Use a typed evaluator with declared attribute types and equality, membership,
existence, prefix, numeric comparison, and semantic-version comparison where
valid. Do not expose scripts or commit stored policies to a vendor syntax. Keep
issuer, keys, audience, and algorithms in verifier configuration; requests
cannot choose trust roots and policy sees only verifier-returned claims.

Retain provenance for every attribute:

- *verified*: issuer, subject, account/project/subscription, instance, image,
  deployment group, region, and equivalent authenticated claims;
- *server-derived*: assurance, credential age, policy version, and values Renga
  computes from trusted context;
- *policy-assigned*: environment, role, owner team, and granted capabilities;
- *self-reported*: hostname, OS facts, requested capabilities, and agent data.

Admission may consume verified and server-derived values. Assigned attributes
are outputs and cannot justify the decision assigning them; they may inform
later runtime decisions. Self-reported values never authorize their reporter.
Policies may map verified claims to assigned values and grant only a subset of
requested capabilities. Security version/image restrictions require verified
artifact identity; self-reported versions are informational only.

Record outcome and stable reason; action, subject, organization, and policy;
immutable policy and verifier versions; issuer and actual verification-key ID or
thumbprint; matched/failed condition IDs; assignments and grants; verifier kind,
assurance, claim provenance, safe evidence digest/reference, and evaluation
time. Return safe denial detail only. Retain enough non-secret material for
reproducible audits, never reusable bearer evidence, plaintext grants, private
keys, or runtime credentials. Keep the decision boundary compatible with a
future shared ABAC engine.

** Verifier Envelope And Initial Path

All verifiers return an envelope containing verifier identity/version, issuer
namespace, subject and cardinality, issuance/expiry, assurance, provenance,
optional instance identity, and typed verifier-specific claims. It is not a
flattened universal claim set. Candidate adapters include generic OIDC workload
identity, SPIFFE X.509/JWT SVID, Kubernetes projected service accounts, AWS
instance/IAM identity, GCP metadata JWT, Azure managed identity, TPM attestation,
one-time manual grants, and operator approval.

The first user-visible automated path is an explicitly configured OIDC workload
identity profile defining trusted issuer, audience, claim schema, and subject
cardinality. Generic OIDC does not promise per-instance attestation. Add
provider-specific adapters after challenge, decision, identity, and credential
lifecycle are stable.

Manual enrollment uses the same protocol. A one-time grant is hashed at rest,
short-lived, authorizes one successful binding, and is consumed only after key
proof; it never becomes the runtime credential. Alternatively, an operator may
approve a short pending-challenge code. Approval creates server-side verified
evidence bound to organization, policy version, challenge, and key; it neither
bypasses policy nor directly creates a Collector. A manual internal test harness
may precede the product path without making manual onboarding the default.

** Credentials, Images, And Scaling

The installation UUID is for display/correlation, not proof. Generate a private
key on first boot; prefer TPM/non-exportable storage and support strictly
permissioned filesystem keys. Runtime credentials must be key-bound, for example
with mTLS, proof-of-possession tokens, or replay-protected signed requests; pick
the mechanism independently of policy.

Installation credentials are separate from =Source.token_hash=; Source remains
the provenance anchor. Migration from MVP bearer tokens requires an explicit
dual-authentication or cutover plan because existing agents lack keys, writable
enrollment state, and renewal. Software cannot conclusively distinguish a full
disk clone containing the same key; fresh evidence, subject binding,
re-attestation, and hardware keys raise assurance, while other deployments
record lower assurance.

Image preparation removes UUIDs, keys, credentials, cached evidence, and lease
state. Golden images contain only the binary, service, server URL, and non-secret
policy selector; first boot generates fresh state. Never embed a shared
permanent token in images, templates, cloud-init, or group variables.

One policy may authorize many autoscaled installations. Every accepted
installation has a unique key and receives one Collector and credential; unique
instance claims exist only when supplied by the verifier. Group subjects require
explicit limits. Policies may constrain active count, rate, images,
accounts/projects/subscriptions, regions, groups, verified artifact identity,
and re-attestation. Provider lifecycle may retire ephemeral Collectors later,
but lease expiry must not implicitly delete them or provenance.

** Policy And Credential Lifecycle

Record the exact enrollment policy version; edits never rewrite history.
Separate admission from continuous eligibility and optionally require fresh
evidence for renewal or administrative re-evaluation. If an installation no
longer qualifies, configurable actions include audit-only, renewal denial,
quarantine, and revocation. Begin with explicit administration and auditability,
not silent revocation after policy edits.

* Milestones

- [ ] Add versioned policies and verifier configurations, challenges, attempts, and structured decisions.
- [ ] Generate installation keypairs and require challenge proof of possession.
- [ ] Add idempotent atomic finalization that consumes an accepted attempt, creates Source+Agent, records the allow decision, and issues a key-bound credential.
- [ ] Add runtime authentication, renewal, and administrative revocation; do not deploy short-lived credentials without renewal.
- [ ] Deliver configured OIDC workload identity as the first user-visible automated path, with image preparation and first-boot enrollment.
- [ ] Add manual grants and operator approval as optional development, static-infrastructure, and break-glass verifiers, not the default path.

- [ ] Add provider-specific and hardware-backed verifier adapters.
- [ ] Revisit a shared ABAC engine only if enrollment requirements demonstrate broader need.

* Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] Accepted automated enrollment atomically creates one existing Source+Agent Collector aggregate and a separate key-bound credential.
- [ ] Retry, singleton replacement, and group-subject behavior follow verifier-declared cardinality and key-bound idempotency rules.
- [ ] Policy decisions use provenance-appropriate attributes and produce safe, reproducible audit records.
- [ ] OIDC is the documented first automated deployment path; manual enrollment remains optional.
- [ ] Images and horizontally scaled installations never share permanent Collector credentials.
- [ ] Renewal, revocation, policy versioning, and MVP credential migration have explicit tested behavior.
